# E9 Scientific Interpretation

## 1. Overall Finding

Within the frozen Exp08 setting, increasing CH overload primarily exposed a latency cost in the strongly CH-dependent Structured strategy rather than a delivery failure. Structured maintained a delivery-ratio mean of 1.000 at every overload factor, with 99 duplicate messages and 198 total forwards, while its mean propagation delay rose monotonically from 4.498 s to 10.523 s. Gossip was unchanged across overload factors because its architecture assigns no CH role and therefore had no direct CH-overload target. DC-SoC and AHBN showed non-monotonic, comparatively small changes in all four aggregate metrics; their means remained close to the Gossip operating point. Thus, Exp08 reveals a reliability-latency-overhead trade-off, not universal superiority by one adaptive method.

## 2. Delivery Ratio

The observed E7 mean sequences for overload factors 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, and 3.0 were:

- Gossip: 0.8305, 0.8305, 0.8305, 0.8305 (95% CI at every factor: [0.8094, 0.8516]).
- Structured: 1.000, 1.000, 1.000, 1.000 (95% CI [1.000, 1.000]).
- DC-SoC: 0.8270, 0.8375, 0.8515, 0.8215 (95% CIs [0.8088, 0.8452], [0.8185, 0.8565], [0.8302, 0.8728], and [0.7949, 0.8481]).
- AHBN: 0.8305, 0.8295, 0.8370, 0.8205 (95% CIs [0.8094, 0.8516], [0.8060, 0.8530], [0.8139, 0.8601], and [0.7962, 0.8448]).

Structured maintained delivery best and exhibited no degradation. Gossip was also stable, although at the lower mean of 0.8305. DC-SoC and AHBN did not degrade monotonically: both rose modestly through factor 2.0 and then had their lowest or near-lowest means at factor 3.0. From factor 1.0 to 3.0, the simple descriptive changes were -0.0055 for DC-SoC and -0.0100 for AHBN. At factor 2.0, DC-SoC had the higher mean (0.8515 versus AHBN's 0.8370); at factor 3.0 their means were nearly equal (0.8215 and 0.8205), and both were below Gossip's 0.8305. These data do not support a claim that AHBN delivered better than every comparator.

## 3. Propagation Delay

Mean delay sequences were 10.015 s at every factor for Gossip; 4.498, 6.023, 7.523, and 10.523 s for Structured; 10.144, 10.284, 10.171, and 10.007 s for DC-SoC; and 10.015, 9.671, 10.533, and 9.929 s for AHBN. Structured therefore showed the clearest sensitivity to the CH delay injection: its mean increased by 6.025 s from factor 1.0 to 3.0. Its 95% CIs were narrow—[4.454, 4.542] s at factor 1.0 and [10.480, 10.566] s at factor 3.0—while delivery remained perfect.

Gossip was invariant because it had no CH target. DC-SoC was comparatively stable around 10 s despite its fixed CH-based hybrid structure. AHBN fluctuated around the same level, attaining its largest mean at factor 2.0 (10.533 s; 95% CI [9.485, 11.582]) rather than factor 3.0. At factor 3.0, Structured had the largest mean delay (10.523 s), followed by Gossip (10.015 s), DC-SoC (10.007 s), and AHBN (9.929 s), although these rankings alone are not formal pairwise significance results. The results indicate that Structured's preservation of full delivery carried an increasing latency cost under CH overload.

## 4. Duplicate Messages

Structured produced 99 duplicates at every factor, substantially fewer than the other strategies. Gossip produced 167.1 throughout. DC-SoC means were 166.4, 168.5, 171.3, and 165.3; AHBN means were 167.1, 166.9, 168.4, and 165.1. The corresponding 95% CIs for DC-SoC ranged from [162.8, 170.0] at factor 1.0 to [160.0, 170.6] at factor 3.0; AHBN ranged from [162.9, 171.3] to [160.2, 170.0].

Increased overload did not cause a monotonic rise in redundancy for Gossip, DC-SoC, or AHBN. DC-SoC and AHBN peaked at factor 2.0 and declined at factor 3.0. Lower duplication was not associated with worse delivery here: Structured combined the fewest duplicates with perfect delivery. Its cost appeared instead in propagation delay as overload increased. Conversely, the roughly 165–171 duplicates generated by Gossip, DC-SoC, and AHBN did not yield delivery means comparable to Structured.

## 5. Total Forwards

Structured used 198 total forwards at every overload factor, whereas Gossip used 249.15 throughout. DC-SoC means were 248.10, 251.25, 255.45, and 246.45; AHBN means were 249.15, 248.85, 251.10, and 246.15. At factor 3.0, the 95% CIs were [238.48, 254.42] for DC-SoC and [238.87, 253.43] for AHBN. As with duplicates, DC-SoC and AHBN rose to factor 2.0 and then declined, rather than increasing monotonically with overload.

Structured was therefore the most communication-efficient strategy by both recorded overhead measures and also delivered every node. Gossip, DC-SoC, and AHBN incurred approximately 48–57 more forwards per run than Structured, yet had lower delivery. High forwarding cost did not purchase the highest reliability in this setting. For Structured, structural efficiency and reliability were accompanied by increasing sensitivity in completion latency, not by delivery degradation.

## 6. Cross-Algorithm Trade-Off

The observed evidence and mechanistic interpretation should be distinguished. Numerically, Structured occupied the strongest reliability/overhead point: delivery 1.000, 99 duplicates, and 198 forwards throughout. Its disadvantage was delay sensitivity, increasing from 4.498 s to 10.523 s as the CH factor rose. This trend is consistent with, but does not by itself prove, the proposed mechanism that its mandatory structured/CH forwarding paths accumulate injected CH latency.

Gossip's constant metrics are consistent with its decentralized static dissemination and lack of a CH-overload target in the frozen experiment. This is architecture-specific immunity to this injection, not evidence of independence from all forms of overload. DC-SoC's fixed DBSCAN clustering, fixed CH selection, fanout 3, inter-fanout 1, and structurally determined forwarding produced stable delay but no clear reliability or overhead advantage over Gossip and AHBN. It must not be interpreted as adaptive. AHBN also remained near the Gossip/DC-SoC operating band. Its changing means and validated internal mode switching indicate adaptive activity, but the aggregate outcomes do not show a decisive external advantage over those two comparators.

## 7. AHBN Adaptive Behaviour

E6 validated 19,985 adaptive-trace rows across all 80 AHBN runs. Runtime latency and utilization observations were finite, non-constant, and varied across overload conditions. The controller made 4,831 dissemination-mode transitions, and every run (80/80) contained at least one transition. There were zero controller-decision consistency mismatches.

Runtime fanout nevertheless remained exactly 3 in every trace row, with zero fanout transitions; this value lies within the frozen bounds [2,4]. Exp08 therefore demonstrates AHBN mode adaptation, not fanout adaptation. Mechanistically, AHBN's external behaviour may be discussed in relation to switching between dissemination modes in response to local observations. However, E7 shows only that AHBN maintained means broadly similar to Gossip and DC-SoC; it does not establish that the mode switching caused those outcomes or that AHBN was universally superior. The evidence supports the narrower claim that AHBN reacted internally under CH-overload conditions while occupying a comparable reliability-latency-overhead operating point to the non-Structured comparators.

## 8. Limitations and Caution

Exp08 tests one CH-overload scenario, topology/workload configuration, and set of frozen implementations and parameters. Its overload targets are architecture-specific: Structured, DC-SoC, and AHBN receive added delay at their respective CH roles, while Gossip constructs no CH and has no direct target. Each condition contains 20 seed-level runs. The Student-t 95% confidence intervals describe uncertainty around sample means; no pairwise hypothesis tests were performed, and interval overlap or separation is not interpreted as formal statistical significance. The results do not isolate the causal contribution of AHBN mode switching, and they should not be generalized beyond this experimental setting without further evidence.

## 9. Final Exp08 Takeaway

Exp08 indicates that CH overload need not reduce delivery when a dissemination structure preserves reachability, but it can shift cost into propagation delay. Structured maintained complete delivery with the lowest duplicate and forwarding counts, while its mean delay rose from 4.498 s to 10.523 s. Gossip was invariant because it had no CH-overload target, and fixed DC-SoC and adaptive AHBN remained near the same lower-delivery, higher-overhead operating band. AHBN demonstrably adapted through dissemination-mode switching in all runs, with fanout fixed at 3, but Exp08 does not show universal performance superiority; it supports a conservative claim of internally responsive mode adaptation with external performance broadly comparable to Gossip and DC-SoC under the frozen conditions.
