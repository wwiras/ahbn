## 3. Exp07 -- Forwarding Fanout Pressure

### Observed results

Values are mean [95% CI].

| Algorithm / condition | Delivery ratio | Propagation delay | Duplicates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gossip k=2 | 0.7320 [0.6947, 0.7693] | 11.7258 [11.1601, 12.2915] | 74.20 [70.47, 77.93] |
| Gossip k=3 | 0.9100 [0.8943, 0.9257] | 9.2524 [8.6648, 9.8400] | 153.15 [149.72, 156.58] |
| Gossip k=4 | 0.9600 [0.9509, 0.9691] | 7.4629 [7.0111, 7.9147] | 201.00 [197.24, 204.76] |
| Gossip k=5 | 0.9700 [0.9609, 0.9791] | 6.4785 [6.0606, 6.8963] | 233.25 [229.21, 237.29] |
| Gossip k=6 | 0.9855 [0.9786, 0.9924] | 5.5893 [5.3682, 5.8103] | 258.35 [254.55, 262.15] |
| AHBN canonical | 0.9100 [0.8943, 0.9257] | 9.2524 [8.6648, 9.8400] | 153.15 [149.72, 156.58] |

### Interpretation

Across the complete Gossip sweep, delivery rises monotonically from 0.7320 at k=2 to 0.9855 at k=6, an absolute increase of 0.2535 (34.63% relative to k=2). Mean delay falls monotonically by 6.1365, from 11.7258 to 5.5893 (52.33%). Duplicates rise monotonically by 184.15, from 74.20 to 258.35 (248.18%). Increasing fanout creates more parallel propagation opportunities and path diversity, improving observed reachability and delay while amplifying redundant receptions. This establishes a forwarding-pressure trade-off, not a formally established optimum.

### AHBN-specific interpretation

The canonical AHBN execution operated at runtime fanout 3 in Exp07; AHBN was not externally swept. Its observed delivery, delay, duplicates, and CIs coincide with Gossip k=3 in the frozen results. This places the canonical AHBN operating point at the k=3 location of the observed trade-off boundary without implying five AHBN fanout conditions or any runtime fanout movement.

### Supported conclusion

Exp07 supports the conservative conclusion that higher Gossip fanout improves observed propagation and delivery at the cost of substantially greater redundancy. Canonical AHBN occupies one observed operating point on that boundary; the experiment does not establish an optimal fanout or universal protocol ranking.

**Gossip.** Its means are invariant across the tested factors. Decentralized redundant path diversity gives the injected cluster-head bottleneck little leverage over its active dissemination paths in this experiment. This is an observed stability under these conditions, not a claim of general immunity.

**Structured.** Delay increases steadily while delivery remains 1.0. Its constrained structural paths and reliance on cluster-head forwarding expose the overloaded role as a localized bottleneck. This is an architectural consequence of the tested structural semantics, not an implementation defect.

**DC-SoC.** Delay increases steadily while delivery remains 1.0. DC-SoC responds through its defined density-based cluster organization, Master/Core roles, and structural forwarding relationships. The Exp08 audit reports that failure/churn-triggered maintenance, reconstruction, repair, and re-clustering were not activated in this control experiment. The response therefore must not be attributed to AHBN-style adaptive fanout.

**AHBN.** Endpoint delay rises modestly and delivery falls modestly, with non-monotonic intermediate means and substantial overlap among several delay and delivery CIs. Runtime evidence records fanout 3--3 with zero fanout transitions, while both cluster and gossip modes and 3,546 mode transitions were observed. Thus the response is interpreted through local observations, controller state, and dissemination-mode/path-selection behavior, not invented fanout escalation. AHBN does not have the lowest delay or highest delivery here.

### Architectural comparison

Exp08 separates decentralized redundant path diversity (Gossip), constrained structural forwarding (Structured), topology/cluster-driven structural dissemination without activated reconstruction in this condition (DC-SoC), and local observation-driven dissemination control with fixed observed fanout but changing modes (AHBN). The evidence shows that architecture determines bottleneck sensitivity; it does not establish a universal numerical winner.

**Gossip.** Duplicates increase monotonically across all five densities while delay decreases monotonically. More neighbors and alternative paths provide faster concurrent propagation and much greater opportunity for duplicate copies.

**Structured.** Duplicates remain exactly zero in these S6 cells and delay is effectively stable. The constrained forwarding structure prevents added physical edges from becoming proportional forwarding multiplicity.

**DC-SoC.** Duplicates remain exactly zero and delay is invariant in the validated means. Density-based clustering and Master/Core structural dissemination determine the active paths; the evidence does not imply that every physical edge is used or that the protocol behaves as unrestricted Gossip.

**AHBN.** Duplicates rise overall, but not monotonically: they reach 194.45 at p=0.10 and are 194.30 at p=0.12. This pattern reflects interaction between a denser physical topology and AHBN's active dissemination policy; it is not evidence that runtime fanout was dynamically reduced. CI overlap is not treated as equivalence.

## 6. Cross-Experiment Synthesis

Exp07 shows that forwarding intensity changes the latency--delivery--duplication boundary. Exp08 shows that protocol architecture determines sensitivity to a localized forwarding bottleneck. Exp09 shows that physical connectivity affects redundancy differently depending on dissemination semantics. Together, the experiments place AHBN as a local observation-driven operating mechanism navigating dissemination trade-offs, not as a protocol required to dominate every static comparator on every metric. Its canonical fanout remained 3 where runtime evidence is available; Exp08 behavior included dissemination-mode changes without fanout changes. The results support mechanism-aware comparison while retaining unfavorable observations, including AHBN's higher delay than several comparators and lower delivery in Exp08.
